Go back home

"friday, bellini"

Martin was the type of man to choose white headphones if presented the choice. He was unremarkable. He did not delay his morning routines. He would stride through them with the same mindless conviction every time. His adventure would start at 4 and end at 5. He was seemingly content, yet barely alive.

On this particular Friday, Martin woke up full of ambition. He was ready for a change. He was ready for the chance to show the other side of Martin – the side who might occasionally consider a light grey, or a navy.

This sense of dizzy enthusiasm was uncommon to Martin but not unknown. There was the time he ran all the way home from school. It took him 14 minutes – ordinarily it would take 32.

And this Friday was one of those Fridays. He brushed his teeth twice. Drank coffee, and then tea. Spent three minutes in the mirror – you are the only lion – and out through the door.

The morning street was whistling. Martin was light. The dust simply did not matter. He was making good time, and the further into his commute he strode, the further he felt he could go. Martin was almost inexplicably content with his chosen action.

When he reached the final crossing he paused. Across the street was a reflective shop window. Martin questions when a window becomes a glass wall. All these shop walls. He recognised the reflection – himself within it. But he did not recognise the bag in his right hand. This bag was beige. Why is the bag beige? Martin considered his shock. Is this because I drank the coffee first? Or is it the navy? Should I know? Why is the bag beige?

The lights changed. Before he could think on it more, he was halfway across the street. Beige bag clutched firmly in hand. Refection removed. Martin bounded to the stairwell. His fob is on a cord, attached to the hoop immediately right of the buckle. Thoughts had eerily subsided as he rushed, beyond enthusiasm, to find a closing door. To the top of the stairs, right, down the corridor – the floors have been over-cleaned – right again, a swing door, a left, shorter corridor – good, less squeaky – left, up three stairs, forwards, right, unlock the door, close the door – lock the door – lock the door.

For a minute Martin was lapsed. He waits, head bowed to the door. Gently he moved backwards, hand outreaching as the door fell coolly out of touch. He chose this room for two reasons. It’s closer than his office. It’s the only other room to which he has the key. He didn’t intentionally keep it, but after five months of not returning, he decided to wait for them to ask. They haven’t.

Seated upon the second shelf, Martin placed the bag between his feet. It’s the same as my bag. But why is the bag beige? The weight, the wear – equal. The handle – equal. The bag – beige. He wouldn’t ask again.

Now for Martin to look without permission is rare. He does not enjoy nor seek the frivolity of daring. He does not ask for, nor give, forgiveness. As it should be or nothing at all. His rules had seen him well.

Today Martin was choosing. He was choosing to find out if there is more to choice than white headphones. He reached to the bag. His finger and thumb pressed each side upon the zipper, and he pulled.

The zip cracked as Martin drew it closer. Martin was opening the bag. The bag is beige. What’s in the bag? All scent is erased from the room. The carpet and the shelves, the folders and the files in between, the rust that smacks the blue chipped paint, and Martin – all replaced. Only the richest, most pungently sweet of fuzzy nectars was now singing to the aroma of the small, cubic space.

Delighted and detailed, each fold of his breath was elongated along the orchard. He drew again, sweeter.

Martin plunged his right hand inside the bag. They were cool. The fuzz tickled him. He rummaged. At least fifteen. The left hand joined the right. Free heaven, what have I done? Both hands emerged, peach grasped. Teeth plunged. Throat gulped. Peach gone. Teeth plunged. Hands drenched. Peach gone. Hands plunged. Teeth plunged. Peach gone. Plunged. Peach gone. Peach gone. Plunged. Peach. Gone.

Three weeks passed before Martin was found. He was alone, naked in the orchard. He’d been living within the branches the whole time. Work had reported his absence when they found the cctv footage of him leaving the storeroom. The farmers had been pleading with him to find reason. He had only reasoned that nothing so perfectly sweet should be kept in beige bags, and that everybody should be free to handpick fruit from the tree. He was handed a fine for trespassing and 70 hours of community service. He would be allowed to begin once omitted from the hospital. They predicted it would take 4 weeks to rebalance the acidity.

Martin didn’t much enjoy peaches. He was far more pear. His white headphones stayed in place, and he chose to keep it that way.
