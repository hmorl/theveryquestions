Go back home

“Icebeing & The Very Questions Rehearsed on Sunday”

Sunday morning. The bells chimed ding-dong descending major scales:

“do ti la so fa me re do, do ti la so fa me re do!”

One of the bell-ringers was clearly out of time, but by the time the sound reached ears hectares away, the resulting resonance turned that imperfection into a pleasant perfection.

“I must set the scene in a curiously musical way”, the narrator insisted.

The weather was a foggy thrum. Light looked cool in it.

Icebeing and his band arose from their slumbers at different times, in different beds. Icebeing woke up at 12, having stayed up until 4am reading Rate Your Music reviews of the Elephant 6 albums. Drew woke up at 8:30, went on a run, and then thought about his interesting guitar noises in preparation for the band’s meet-up later:

“What if I fed the signal from my delay pedal straight into my Amex Gold account, converted the rewards points into Airmiles, sent the sound flying, inverted it on return, turned it back into a signal, and chaperoned it to the amplifier via the Digitech? Will the band appreciate that? It’ll save me cash, that’s for sure.”

Harry woke up at 8 and put on his glasses. Iwan woke up bang on 9:30 and exercised. On a cardiovascular level, it’s either him or Drew who are winning, I expect.

Eve snoozed until around 10:36 and then scared the local cats away with her persistent, but ornate, saxophone honking that she has become quite famous for. She had a lot to practice and little time to do it in, for the Thameslink beckoned and she had been up late on previous nights, running the industry.

2 o’ clock was here before everyone knew it. Well, not everyone. Alice, dear friend of the band, did know it. She was really eager to kick Iwan out of the Yaris as soon as it became stationary (could she get away with jettisoning him at a slight crawl?), because shops were closing at 4 and she had a lot to do as well. Iwan respected this and was ready to go.

Everyone else arrived a bit late and that was OK, it seemed.

The rehearsal commenced in medias res, like Star Wars, but without the war. The newer stuff top-loaded their upcoming Congratulations support set, so the band warmed up with the stuff they knew best. A certain amount of warming was needed, as it had been a while.

One of the newer ones was actually an oldie, entitled They’re Holding; a cut from Striped (2020). They had played it before, many suns and moons ago, but Icebeing decided to revive it and change some things to make it sound more gnarly.

Near the end of the song, Icebeing decided to lead the band through an interesting free improvisation section. He touched his iPad which made it do glitchy sounds, signalling to the others that they should “wig out” and do whatever they pleased for a bit.

During this section, Eve made mistakes on purpose because when everyone else is making noises and nasty sounds like feedback and distortion, this is what you can do on a wind instrument and it sounds acceptable in context. Harry fuzzed off on the bass, and ear drums were pushed to their limits. “I should dial that back a bit next time”, he regretted.

Iwan became VERY ERRATIC! with his hits. He experimented with hitting parts of the drums that you don’t usually hit at drum school, like the sides, the clamps, and the wingnuts.

And from Drew’s guitar emerged a micro-economy of sound. A late-stage scheme of tonal structure, pyramidic in form, rose from the ashes of a previous civilisation. It told its friends. It grew up greeting with “hey buddy”, selling watches from inside the jacket, then moved onto flipping parcels on eBay in later life. The sound was all-droning, all-racketeering and its melodic special offers formed into a sonic reflection of society, that would have withstood the most rigorous of analyses. Wow. The Amex trick worked, and everyone was seriously impressed.

But it was at that moment the walls turned pink, and the rehearsal took a turn for the weird and the eerie. Drew’s experimentations in guitar and loyalty cards had broken something fundamental.

While everybody was distracted by the noise and the “art”, the door to the live room opened inconspicuously and a bunch of balloons wandered in.

The colourful slime that followed them would have certainly been interesting to look at, but it wreaked havoc on everyone’s instruments. Drums were dulled, saxophones were stickied, tambourine zills rusted. The Icebeing Rig ™️ coughed and spluttered digitally, and Harry’s bass was ill-defined and horrid. Drew’s guitar maintained, and kept selling. For several minutes, it even tracked the Dow Jones and had its own ticker symbol (DREW). A bubble to burst?

There was a piecewise realisation of the band during this episode. Harry noticed something was awry first, but his jazz tendencies enabled him to embrace it, albeit through clinched teeth.

Iwan became 2D, which was not such a remarkable transformation due to his existing form, but he did widen considerably like a pin-rolled dough sheet. He quickly hugged the wall and then spun round the room like an accelerated particle, and the drums went with.

The balloons caught wind of the cooling fan in the room, got caught, and started to nod violently. At this point, the sound was a deep whir containing all frequencies in varying measures. Some of the balloons popped under the stress, like cannons, and their rubber corpses joined doughlike-Iwan in the spinning.

Icebeing, unlike anyone else, had a beaming smile throughout, which was extremely in-character for him. “This is sick”, he uttered in slow-motion. Then, the dots and stripes on his vintage jumper turned into dots and staves which seeped off the fabric and into the air. Sailor’s Hornpipe was now manuscripted in full and airborne, and it rang out like this morning’s chimes, flying in contrary motion to Iwan.

Bloody hell. The band weren’t at Groove Gardens any more, nor was this Wembley. “Groove” became “woozed”, and the “Gardens” became “mind and body”. Descartes became drunk.

“Where is this all going?”, the narrator started to worry. The original vision for this story had been tarnished by a colourful, yet revisionist rework, designed to paint the band in an interesting light. Why do they do what they do, why do they feel licensed to subvert the typical act of a band (play some songs), play with forms in a gimmicky way, and generate “artwork” once a month? Surely that just portrays delusions of grandeur?

“This was just meant to be a simple account and progress update, and now I must conclude a stupid psychedelic tale”, the narrator uffed, challenged.

Here goes nothing.

From all of the chaos, confusion, and sonic rubble, a THX D-MAJOR CHORD instantiated and everyone turned their dumbfounded ears to it. Desperation turned to hope, and all became clear, to everyone, even me. I get it now.

We must all mark time in interesting ways, so as to not drift through the day to day, the jobs, the needing of things, towards the end of life. It shall slow time to demarcate its passing. To do that with cool noises, some jokes, funny words and interesting chords, are the cherries that sweeten the journey.

And just like the moral of the story, the THX D-MAJOR SOUND marked the end of rehearsal, and everything sonic came to a halt. The room was restored. There were 10 minutes to go, actually.

“Shall we play that one again since we’ve got time?” Iwan said.

“Yeah, I messed up in the second verse”, admitted Harry.
